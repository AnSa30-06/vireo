Ledgerline 1.3.0

I cloned what Cobi sells, and fixed what it does.

Ledgerline reads your customer spreadsheets and hands you a short list of things to act on this week. Every item carries the evidence that produced it, an owner, a due date and a status. If it cannot answer you, it says so instead of inventing a number.

What I found when I read all 66 pages of their documentation

Their marketing says

“AI Decision Stack for Customer Intelligence. Cobi reads their actual behaviour and shows you exactly what to do about it.”

Their documentation says

“An AI-powered data analytics platform that lets you ask questions about your data in plain English, no SQL required.”

What I took from them

Their surface is good, and most of it is what people pay for. I built all of it.

  1. Ask in plain EnglishType a question about your customers, get an answer back.
  2. Saved segmentsGroups of customers built from rules. Describe a group in words and it writes the rules for you.
  3. Metrics, charts and dashboardsSave a number, put it on a board, come back to it.
  4. StoriesA report you can run again, on a weekly schedule, and export.
  5. An embeddable question boxPut it on another website with one line of HTML.
  6. Data scopesA key can only read certain tables, and only the rows belonging to one customer.
  7. Customer state labelsAt risk, Dormant, Power user, Upsell ready, and the rest.
  8. The dot gridTheir single best idea. One dot is one person, so “36 of 120” is a picture.

What I changed

Their method is measured to fail on real data. These are the differences that matter.

  1. No free-form SQLTheir approach scores 86% in tests and 0–6% on real company databases. Ledgerline uses a fixed list of nine questions, each with a query written by hand.
  2. “I cannot answer that” is a real answerIt refuses, and shows you what it checked. A wrong number that looks right does more damage than ten right ones repair.
  3. Every number opens onto its evidenceOne click from the figure to the rows it counted.
  4. A decision is a record, not an alertIt has an owner, a due date, a status and an outcome. It comes back if you ignore it.
  5. Definitions live next to the numberWrong figure? Edit what it counted, right there. Cobi makes you leave and edit a block of prose.
  6. A scope cannot widen quietlyTheir own docs admit deleting a scope exposes more data. Here, widening one names the live keys and asks first.
  7. Charts say what they left outA capped chart prints how many points are missing, so the picture never quietly disagrees with the number above it.
  8. It works with no internet and no AI modelAnswers fall back to keyword matching, and say that is what happened.
